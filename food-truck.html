Full route · 5 steps

Start a food truck on Guam

Most of this happens in one place: the Business License and Permit Center, where Rev & Tax sends your application to each agency for sign-off.

Source research: October 4, 2026. Older or unconfirmed requirements are marked below. Confirm current requirements with the agency.

You will end up with
  • A business license (Rev & Tax)
  • A sanitary permit for a mobile food service establishment (Public Health)
  • A health certificate for each person handling food
  • Fire Department clearance, plus an LP-gas permit if your setup needs one
  1. 1

    Apply for your business license

    Rev & Tax, General Licensing and Registration Branch · 671-635-1828/9 · businesslicense@revtax.guam.gov

    • Fill out Form BL-01, print it, and sign it in ink (digital signatures are not accepted)
    • Business location must include lot number, block number, street address and village, or it is rejected
    • Describe the activity in detail, for example "RETAIL: sale of prepared food from a mobile food truck"
    • Attach the Good Moral Questionnaire and a copy of your photo ID
    • Using a business name (DBA)? Call or email to check it is available, and bring the DBA form notarized
    Form BL-01 →Instructions + questionnaire →Rev & Tax procedure →
    Not yet confirmed: Rev & Tax's fee sheet lists license fees from $100 to $1,000 based on gross receipts, and a late fee after June 30 of $50 plus $1 a day. The sheet is dated March 2013 and may be outdated (fee sheet, 2013). Confirm with Rev & Tax.
  2. 2

    Wait for agency clearances

    Routed by Rev & Tax through the permit center

    • Rev & Tax reviews your application, then sends it to each agency that must clear it
    • For businesses involving food, expect Land Management, the Fire Department, and Public Health
    • Agencies may contact you to schedule an inspection, and some charge their own fee
    • When every clearance is endorsed, Rev & Tax contacts you to pay and pick up the license
  3. 3

    Get your sanitary permit

    Public Health (DPHSS), Division of Environmental Health, Plans and Specifications Section at the permit center

    • Guam law makes it unlawful to run a mobile food service establishment without a valid sanitary permit
    • New establishments file Form A (sanitary application) and Form B (food establishment supplement)
    • The permit is issued after plan review and an inspection, and it cannot be moved to another person or establishment
    • Fee: $568 a year from January 1, 2026 (up from $468). It covers 1 to 10 employees, plus $5 for each additional employee, and is pro-rated to your renewal date (DPHSS release PR26-044, Dec 30, 2025)
    Sanitary permit application (archived) →Form B (archived) →Environmental Health →(archived copy) →
    DPHSS is rebuilding its website, so the DPHSS form links on this page are archived copies (the sanitary permit application was archived July 2026; Form B is the 2021 version). Confirm you have the current forms with DEH at (671) 922-2533, the number printed on the sanitary permit application.
  4. 4

    Health certificates for everyone handling food

    Public Health (DPHSS)

    • Each food handler applies for a health certificate
    • Study the food safety course manual first
    Health certificate application (archived) →Study manuals, 6 languages (archived) →
  5. 5

    Fire Department clearance

    Guam Fire Department, Fire Prevention Bureau · 671-646-8810

    • The Fire Department is one of the clearing agencies on retail and food business licenses
    • Guam follows the International Fire Code, which requires an operational permit to store and use LP-gas, with some exceptions
    • Ask the bureau whether your propane setup needs its own permit and inspection
    GFD forms →
  6. +

    Selling at a fiesta, fair, or one-off event?

    • Every food seller at an island event needs a temporary food service establishment (TFSE) sanitary permit, applied for at DEH's Plans and Specifications Section at the permit center

  7. +

    Where can the truck park and sell?

    Not yet confirmed: we found no Guam rule on where food trucks may park. Owners report negotiating spot by spot with village mayors, Parks and Recreation, DPW, and DPHSS. Reported fees include Parks and Recreation at $1,500 a month plus $5 per employee, the Tumon Night Market at $175 a night, and village festivals from $200 to $6,000 (Isla Public, Sep 22, 2026). Ask each site owner before you set up.
    Not yet confirmed: we did not find whether a truck must use a commissary kitchen. That rule would be in the Guam Food Code (26 GAR Division 1, Chapter 4). Ask DPHSS DEH.
