Build a house, addition, or fence

New construction, additions, and fences go through one permit at Public Works. Here is what the Building Law asks for, in order.

You will end up with
  • A DPW building permit, cleared by the agencies at the Business License and Permit Center
  • Passed DPW inspections
  • A certificate of occupancy before anyone moves in (new buildings and changes of use)
  1. 1

    Confirm your lot, zoning, and boundaries

    Department of Land Management, Division of Planning

    • Know your lot, tract, block, and zoning district (the permit application asks for all four)
    • The plot plan in step 2 must be drawn from an accurate boundary-line survey
    • If what you want to build does not fit your zoning, you need a zone change or variance first
    DLM Planning →
  2. 2

    Prepare plans and a plot plan

    You, your designer, or a licensed architect or engineer

    • Plans: at least two copies of plans and specifications, drawn to scale, showing the work
    • Plot plan: to scale, showing the new construction, every existing structure, distances from lot lines, and street grades
    • DPW may ask for structural, mechanical, and electrical details. Engineering plans must be signed by the responsible engineer or architect
    • For minor work, the building official may waive the plans or the plot plan
  3. 3

    File the permit application

    DPW Building Inspection and Permits Section, at the Business License and Permit Center

    • Fill out the Application for Permit and Plan Review: check New Building, Add, or Fence Wall
    • Give the cost of improvements, construction type, sewage (public or septic), water supply, and scope of work
    • The owner or lessee signs, along with the contractor (with license number) and the architect or engineer (with seal number)
    • In a flood zone, attach a topographic map and a flood elevation certificate
    • Someone applying for the owner needs the owner's verified affidavit
    Permit application →Permit center →Check a contractor →
  4. 4

    Pay the fee and start within 3 months

    Paid to the Treasurer of Guam

    • Fees follow Table 3-A of the Uniform Building Code, based on the total value of the work as set by DPW
    • Homes pay half: a single-family house, fence, storage, or other residential improvement pays half the table fee
    • The permit lapses if work does not start within 3 months, or stops for 3 months. Extensions must be in writing
    The law sets how fees are calculated, not a dollar amount. Ask DPW for the fee on your valuation.
  5. 5

    Pass inspections and get your certificate of occupancy

    DPW building inspectors

    • Schedule DPW inspections as work progresses, and keep the permit and approved plans on site
    • No new building may be used until DPW issues a certificate of occupancy and it is posted on the premises
    • Additions that change a building's use need a new certificate of occupancy too
  6. +

    Clearing land or putting in a septic tank?

    • Clearing, grading, and septic systems need Guam EPA review as well. See the clearing and grading route
