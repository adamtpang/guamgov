# Hold an event, fiesta, or night market booth

Topic id: `events`. Page: https://permitgu.vercel.app/events

Which steps you need depends on what you are doing. Selling food? Start with step 1. Using a park? Step 2. Closing a road? Step 3. Skip the steps that do not apply to you.

## You may end up with

- A temporary food service establishment (TFSE) sanitary permit for each event location, if you sell food (Public Health)
- A park reservation, if you use a government park (Parks and Recreation)
- Sign-off from the village mayor, Public Works, and Police, if you close a road or hold a parade
- Fire Department clearance for tents, cooking, and crowds
- A temporary liquor license, if you serve alcohol (Rev & Tax, ABC Board)

## Step 1: Selling food? Get a temporary food permit for each event

Who: Public Health (DPHSS), Division of Environmental Health, Plans and Specifications Section at the Business License and Permit Center, Upper Tumon · (671) 922-2533

- Every food seller at an island event needs a Temporary Food Service Establishment (TFSE) sanitary permit
- You need a separate permit for the location of each event
- Apply as early as you can so the permit is issued before the event
- Selling without a valid permit can lead to a fine and closure of your booth

UNCONFIRMED: Not yet confirmed: the rules above come from a Guam Daily Post report of a DPHSS notice (Nov 14, 2022, updated Feb 2025). It lists office hours of Monday, Wednesday and Friday, 8 a.m. to noon and 1 to 5 p.m., and the numbers 671-646-1276 and 671-300-9570/75. The TFSE form is a 2019 file kept by archive.org because DPHSS is rebuilding its website. We did not find the TFSE fee or how many days ahead to apply. Confirm the current form, fee, and hours with DEH at (671) 922-2533.

## Step 2: Using a park or beach? Reserve it with Parks and Recreation

Who: Department of Parks and Recreation (DPR), Parks Division · 671-475-6288 · inquiries@dpr.guam.gov · #1 Paseo De Susana, Hagåtña

- A reservation guarantees your location, date, and time
- Reservations open three months before the event
- Phone hours are 8 a.m. to noon and 1 to 5 p.m. In person, 8 a.m. to noon and 1 to 3 p.m. Closed weekends and holidays
- You pay for any damage, must clean up all trash before your time ends, may only use fire in the BBQ pits, and may not tie banners, ropes or balloons to trees or historic structures
- DPR's fee schedule lists: special events $500, temporary vendor (3 days max) $100 ($50 for nonprofits), annual park vendor $1,500, large pavilion $75 a day, small pavilion $15 a day, open area or canopy $10 a day, amphitheater $125 a day, event parking $50 an hour
- Refundable cleaning deposit by crowd size: $75 (up to 99 people), $150 (100 to 249), $250 (250 to 499), $500 (500 to 1,000), $1,000 (over 1,000)

UNCONFIRMED: Not yet confirmed: DPR's fee schedule page shows no effective date, and we did not find a downloadable permit form. Food truck owners told Isla Public that Parks and Recreation charged $1,500 a month plus $5 per employee, which does not match the $1,500 a year on DPR's page (Isla Public, Sep 22, 2026 (https://www.islapublic.org/news/2026-09-22/food-truck-owners-say-business-is-hindered-by-widely-varying-fees-inconsistent-regulations)). Ask DPR which fee applies to you.

Sources:

- [DPR fee schedule](https://dpr.guam.gov/fee-schedule/)

## Step 3: Closing a road, or holding a parade or run

Who: Your village mayor, the Department of Public Works (DPW), and the Guam Police Department

- Talk to the mayor of each village on your route first
- Ask DPW and the Police what permit and traffic control your route needs

UNCONFIRMED: Not yet confirmed: we did not find a current official page or form for a road closure, parade, or road race permit from DPW or the Guam Police Department. Search results describe an older bill (Bill 468-31) that would let mayors close village roads for funerals and fiestas without a DPW highway encroachment permit, but we could not open the bill or confirm it became law. Ask your mayor and DPW what applies today.

## Step 4: Village fiestas and events through the mayor's office

Who: Your village mayor's office · Mayors' Council of Guam

- Village fiestas, festivals, and night markets are often run or approved by the mayor's office, so ask there about booth space and booth fees
- The Mayors' Council of Guam lists each village mayor's office

UNCONFIRMED: Not yet confirmed: these booth fees come from food truck owners quoted in the news, not from any official fee schedule: Malesso' festival $6,000 for 2.5 days, Hagat $3,000, Inalåhan $200 a weekend, and the Tumon Night Market $175 a night (Isla Public, Sep 22, 2026 (https://www.islapublic.org/news/2026-09-22/food-truck-owners-say-business-is-hindered-by-widely-varying-fees-inconsistent-regulations)). Fees vary by village and event. Ask the organizer or mayor's office.

Sources:
- [Mayors' Council of Guam](https://mcog.guam.gov/)

## Step 5: Tents, cooking, crowds, and sound: check with the Fire Department

Who: Guam Fire Department, Fire Prevention Bureau · 671-646-8810

- Guam follows the International Fire Code, which requires an operational permit for things like LP-gas, with some exceptions
- Ask the bureau whether your tents, propane cooking, generators, or crowd size need a permit or inspection
- The Fire Department's forms page has a permit application, a self-inspection form, and its fee schedule

UNCONFIRMED: Not yet confirmed: we did not find a Guam form or rule specific to event tents or to amplified sound (loudspeakers, DJs, live bands). The GFD permit forms are dated 2013 to 2018 and the fee schedule file appears to date from 2008. Ask GFD about tents, and ask DPR or your mayor about sound limits for your location.

Sources:
- [GFD forms](https://gfd.guam.gov/forms/)

## Step 6: Serving or selling alcohol

Who: Alcoholic Beverage Control (ABC) Board, Department of Revenue and Taxation

- Ask Rev & Tax about a temporary alcoholic beverage license for your event before you plan any alcohol sales

UNCONFIRMED: Not yet confirmed: ABC Board meeting agendas on guamtax.com show event organizers applying for temporary on-sale beer licenses, each naming the dates, hours, and a fenced or cordoned area where people may drink, but we did not find the current application form, fee, or lead time (ABC agenda, Apr 2016 (https://www.guamtax.com/abc/ABCAgenda-04-27-2016.pdf)). Applications appear to go before the Board at its meetings, so start early. Confirm with Rev & Tax.

## Step 7: Fireworks

Who: Guam Fire Department

- Do not plan fireworks without talking to the Fire Department first

UNCONFIRMED: Not yet confirmed: in December 2023, officials told KUAM that residents still could not set off their own fireworks, because GFD had not yet issued rules for the law that legalized consumer fireworks (KUAM, Dec 28, 2023 (https://kuam.com/story/50287428/can-the-community-set-off-their-own-fireworks-now-officials-say-no)). Recent New Year's shows were run by professional pyrotechnic companies (Isla Public, Dec 30, 2025 (https://www.islapublic.org/news/2025-12-30/gvb-to-host-two-fireworks-shows-on-nye)). We did not find a current public display permit process. Ask GFD.
