# Repair typhoon damage

Topic id: `typhoon-repair`. Page: https://permitgu.vercel.app/typhoon-repair

Roof torn, walls damaged, shutters gone. First find out whether your repair needs a permit at all. Small owner-done fixes often do not.

## The short answer, from Guam's Building Law (21 GCA Chapter 66)

- No permit needed: you, your family, or friends (not a hired contractor) replace 25% or less of your roof covering in a 12-month period
- No permit needed: owner-done, nonstructural repairs like painting, drywall, siding, or interior finishing, up to 25% of the building's value in 12 months
- Permit needed: you hire a contractor, the repair is structural, or it goes beyond those limits
- Rebuild to new-building code: if repairs in 12 months go over 50% of the building's value

## Step 1: Check which side of the line you are on

Who: 21 GCA §66104, Application to Existing Buildings

- Estimate the repair cost against the value of the building
- Replacing more than 25% of the roof covering means the new roof must meet the code for new buildings
- Any structural repair (roof framing, beams, load-bearing walls) needs a permit, even if you do it yourself

## Step 2: Hire a licensed contractor

Who: Contractors License Board · 671-649-9676

- Check the contractor on the board's published listing before you pay anything
- Check the suspended and revoked list too, which scam crews often end up on after big storms
- Make sure their license covers your kind of work, for example roofing or typhoon shutters

Sources:
- [Contractors listing](https://clb.guam.gov/contractors-listing/)
- [Suspended or revoked](http://clb.guam.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Suspended-Contractors.pdf)

## Step 3: Apply for the building permit

Who: DPW Building Inspection and Permits Section, at the Business License and Permit Center

- Fill out the Application for Permit and Plan Review and check "Repair" or "Reconstructed"
- Give the lot, tract, and block, the zoning district, the cost of improvements, and the scope of work
- The owner or lessee signs, along with your contractor (with license number) and any architect or engineer
- If the property is in a flood zone, attach a topographic map and a flood elevation certificate
- Roofing counts in the valuation that sets your permit fee, and fees double if work starts before the permit (21 GCA §66408)
- Someone applying for the owner must include the owner's verified affidavit authorizing them

Sources:
- [Permit application](https://bsp.guam.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/DPW-Application-for-Permit-and-Plan-Review-Fillable.pdf)

- [21 GCA Chapter 66](https://col.guamcourts.gov/sites/default/files/21gc066_Q.pdf)

## Step 4: Build to code and pass inspection

Who: DPW building inspectors

- Schedule DPW inspections as work progresses
- Keep your permit and approved plans on site

## Also: After a declared disaster

- Guam Homeland Security / Office of Civil Defense posts emergency updates, including any DPW permit changes and relief programs
- The only disaster permit exemption in the law is for government emergency utility and environmental work (21 GCA §66202.1(b)), not private homeowners
- After Typhoon Mawar, we found no executive order waiving permits for private repairs (EO 2023-02 did not)
- The free RISEUP temporary roof program after Mawar closed on June 30, 2023
