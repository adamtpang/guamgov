# Check zoning or change how land is used

Topic id: `zoning`. Page: https://permitgu.vercel.app/zoning

Every lot on Guam has a zone that decides what you can build and how close to the lot lines. If your plan does not fit, the Guam Land Use Commission can grant a variance, a zone change, or a conditional use. Here is how that works under the Zoning Law.

## You will end up with

- Your lot's zone and the rules that come with it
- If needed, a written decision from the Guam Land Use Commission (or, for small setback requests, the DLM Director), recorded at Land Management and Public Works
- A zoning OK that lets DLM endorse your building permit

## Step 1: Find your lot and its zone

Who: Department of Land Management (DLM), Division of Planning

- Have your lot, block, and tract numbers and your certificate of title number ready. Every zoning form asks for them
- Guam LandWeb is DLM's online land records system. It searches parcels, parcel history, certificates of title, and survey plans, with user guides and training videos
- The official Zoning Map and every recorded amendment are open to public inspection during normal business hours (21 GCA §61646)
- DLM can issue a Certification of Zoning for $10 (21 GCA §61660)
- Division of Planning: (671) 649-5263 ext. 375, 590 S. Marine Corps Drive, 3rd Floor, ITC Building, Tamuning

## Step 2: Know what your zone allows

Who: 21 GCA Chapter 61, the Zoning Law of Guam

- Zones: A (agricultural or rural), R1 (one-family), R2 (multiple dwelling), P (parking), C (commercial), M1 (light industrial), M2 (industrial), and LC (limited commercial) (§61201). The law also sets H (resort-hotel), S-1 (school), and PF (public facility) zones (§§61311 to 61313)
- Single-family setbacks: front 15 ft, rear 10 ft, side 8 ft. Minimum lot is 5,000 sq ft, or 10,000 sq ft in the rural A zone (§61501)
- Some uses are allowed in a zone only as a conditional use, which needs Commission approval of your site plan (§61303)
- If the use or the building does not fit, you have three paths: a variance (step 3), a zone change (step 4), or a conditional use (step 4)

Sources:
- [21 GCA Chapter 61 (zoning)](https://col.guamcourts.gov/sites/default/files/21gc061_Q.pdf)

## Step 3: Ask for a variance if your lot cannot meet the rules

Who: Guam Land Use Commission (GLUC), or the DLM Director for minor setbacks

- Minor setback variance: for a single-family home or a residential accessory structure, the DLM Director can approve up to 3 ft into one yard (front, side, or rear). You need the neighbors' concurrence on the closest property line, roof runoff must stay on your lot, and no easement can be crossed. DLM notifies adjoining owners, who get 30 days to object (§61616(l))
- Zone variance: the Commission can vary height, yards, lot size, density, or parking where a lot's size, shape, or topography makes the rules a real hardship (§61616)
- The Commission must find all four: a practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship, exceptional conditions on your property, no harm to the neighborhood, and no conflict with the Master Plan (§61617)
- The village's Municipal Planning Council holds at least two public meetings and sends the Commission a resolution for or against (§61623)

Sources:

## Step 4: Apply for a zone change or conditional use

Who: Filed with the GLUC Executive Secretary, care of the DLM Division of Planning

- Zone change: any owner or lessee in the area can apply. The Commission must find that public necessity, convenience, and general welfare justify it (§§61630, 61631)
- The zone change form asks for a one-page justification letter, a zoning map out to 1,000 ft, a land-use map out to 750 ft, a parcel map out to 500 ft, your recorded property map, and an as-built plan
- Summary zone change: owners of A land can ask to rezone to R1 or R2, and owners of R1 land to R2, through a faster DLM procedure. The Director decides within 60 calendar days, then the Legislature has 45 days to act (§61639)
- Conditional use: the Commission reviews your site plan, including sewage, access, parking, building size, and the effect on neighbors. Its conditions run with the land forever (§61303)
- The Commission decides a zone change within 40 days of the hearing. If approved, it goes to the Governor, and approved changes are recorded at DLM and DPW (§61634)

## Step 5: Give public notice, attend the hearing, and pay fees

Who: You, DLM, and the Commission

- Sign: put up a sign on the lot at least 4 ft by 8 ft. For zone changes and variances it reads "Notice to Rezone" in red letters at least 12 in high, and goes up within 5 days of filing (§§61619, 61633)
- Mail: for zone changes and conditional uses, owners within 500 ft of the lot are notified. For zone changes, a newspaper notice runs at least 10 days before the hearing, and the village mayor is told (§61633). For conditional uses, notice goes out at least 10 days before the hearing (§61303)
- Missing a notice step makes any approval null and void (§§61303, 61619, 61633)
- Hearings are held in your village, and at least one variance hearing must be after 6:00 p.m. (§61619)
- Filing fees: zone change $50, conditional use $50, zone variance $75, setback variance $75, summary or split zone change $25, appeal $50, plus $0.25 a page after the first 5 pages (§61660)
- You also pay the cost of mailing notices (postage, paper, envelopes, research, equipment) and the newspaper ad at its current rate (§§61303(d), 61660(b))

## Also: Lot on the shoreline?

Who: Guam Seashore Protection Commission (the same members as the GLUC)

- Development in the seashore reserve needs a seashore clearance from the Commission, on top of any other permit. The reserve runs inland from the mean high water line, generally 10 meters (21 GCA §§63103, 63108)
- The Commission must find no substantial harm to the environment, and you carry the burden of proof

## Also: Disagree with a decision?

- Variances: anyone aggrieved can ask the Superior Court of Guam to review a Commission variance decision within 15 days after it is filed at DLM and DPW (§61621)
- Permit refusals: the Commission hears appeals claiming the Building Official made an error refusing a building permit or certificate of occupancy under the Zoning Law. The appeal fee is $50 (§§61615, 61660)
