Clear, grade, or install septic

Moving earth or putting in a septic tank brings in Guam EPA and Public Works, plus sign-offs from the utilities and land offices.

You will end up with
  • A Guam EPA Clearing, Grubbing, Grading and Stockpiling permit
  • A DPW clearing and grading permit, signed off by the agencies listed on it
  • For septic: Guam EPA clearance on your building permit, and a passed water-tightness test
  • A Fire Department burning permit, if you plan to burn cleared brush
  1. 1

    Apply to Guam EPA

    Guam EPA · 3304 Mariner Ave #17, Barrigada · 671-588-4751

    • Guam EPA issues the Clearing, Grubbing, Grading and Stockpiling permit
    • Apply online through the Guam EPA permit portal, or find the current form on the forms page
    • Importing soil or fill? The forms page lists soil importation requirements too
    Apply online →Guam EPA forms →
  2. 2

    Get the DPW clearing and grading permit

    DPW Division of Building Permits and Inspection

    • The application asks for district, block, lot, tract, lot area, estimated cubic yards of cut and fill, soil description, start and finish dates, and contractor license number
    • DPW routes it for approval by Land Management (zoning), Parks and Recreation, the Guam Power Authority, Guam EPA, the telephone authority, and the DLM Survey Division
    • No burning on site unless the Fire Department has issued a burning permit
    • Government agencies need this permit too, the same as private owners (21 GCA §66202.1)
    DPW clearing and grading form →Permit center →
    The form posted online is an older version. Ask at the permit center whether a newer one is in use, and who handles the telephone sign-off now.
  3. 3

    Building with a septic tank? Follow the 2024 rules

    Guam EPA, under Public Law 37-105 (effective July 18, 2024)

    • Septic tanks may no longer be built from hollow block (CMU)
    • Concrete tanks must be pre-cast or poured monolithic, at least 4000 psi at 28 days
    • Inlet and outlet must be at least 6 feet apart, with the inlet at least 3 inches above the outlet
    • Every tank must pass a water-tightness test that Guam EPA observes: fill, hold 24 hours, refill, and hold for 1 hour
    • No occupancy permit is issued until the tank passes
